# Crop Diagnosis — Lessons Learned

## What Worked

### Disease
- **Viral Infections:** Identify by systemic morphological distortions (upward/inward curling, puckering/rugosity, "shoestring" narrowing, and rosetting).
- **Mosaic Viruses:** Look for asymmetric yellow-green mottling and blotchy chlorosis.
- **Powdery Mildew:** Identify by a superficial, white, flour-like or dusty coating on upper leaf surfaces.
- **Downy Mildew:** Look for angular yellow lesions.
- **Fungal Blights:** 
    - **Early Blight:** Small, discrete brown spots with concentric "target" rings and yellow halos.
    - **Late Blight:** Large, irregular, water-soaked necrotic patches.
- **Other Fungal Markers:** Raised orange-tinted pustules (rust) and sunken, dark brown to black necrotic lesions (anthracnose).
- **Vascular Wilts:** Identify by systemic failure, such as the complete collapse and browning of individual plants in a patchy distribution.

### Pest
- **Chewing Insects:** Look for physical tissue loss (ragged holes, shredded margins, "shot holes") and brownish, granular frass in the plant whorl.
- **Sucking Pests (Aphids/Mealybugs):** Identify by visible insect clusters or white, cottony, waxy deposits along midribs and new growth.
- **Thrips/Mites:** Look for fine-grained surface damage (pale yellowish-white pinpoint stippling, silvery-metallic shimmering, or fine webbing).
- **Secondary Indicators:** Grayish-brown "sooty mold" coatings and the presence of ants.
- **Stem Borers:** Identify by "whiteheads" (bleached, empty panicles).

### Nutrient Deficiency
- **General Chlorosis:** Uniform pale yellow-green or washed-out hue across leaves.
- **Marginal Necrosis:** Brown, scorched leaf tips and edges contrasting with a green interior.
- **Interveinal Chlorosis:** Yellowing concentrated at leaf margins while primary veins remain dark green.
- **Structural Signs:** Severely stunted growth or loose, uneven formation of crop organs.
- **Exclusionary Markers:** Absence of localized lesions, black spots, or insect-chewed holes.

### Healthy
- **Visual Markers:** Uniform deep green foliage, intact leaf margins, and balanced growth architecture (strong stems, normal fruit size).
- **Reproductive Organs:** Plump seed pods, vibrant flowers, and full, unbleached spikes.
- **Environmental Distinction:** Distinguish surface dust from powdery mildew and cracked soil/drooping from pathology.

## What Didn't Work

### Disease
- **Prioritize Structure over Color:** Do not rely on yellowing to diagnose viruses; prioritize upward curling, cupping, and leathery textures for Leaf Curl viruses.
- **Texture vs. Pigmentation:** Distinguish white, flour-like surface residues (Powdery Mildew) from internal pigmentation changes (chlorosis or stippling).
- **Lesion Geometry:** Use specific shapes to differentiate pathogens: concentric rings (blights), spindle-shaped/eye-shaped spots (Blast), or angular spots bounded by veins (Downy Mildew).
- **Turgidity vs. Localized Decay:** Distinguish systemic drooping and asymmetrical wilting (Vascular Wilts) from localized necrotic spots (Blight/Root Rot).

### Pest
- **Curling Direction:** Distinguish upward cupping/twisting (Thrips/Aphids) from downward, brittle cupping (Mites).
- **Tissue Loss vs. Necrosis:** Differentiate physical tissue removal (ragged holes/missing chunks) from the "shot-hole" effect where necrotic spots fall out.
- **Localized vs. Systemic Collapse:** Identify "dead heart" (localized collapse of the terminal shoot) as a borer sign, not a systemic vascular wilt.
- **Surface Residues:** Do not mistake waxy pest secretions or honeydew-related sooty mold for fungal spores or powdery mildew.

### Nutrient Deficiency
- **Gradient Analysis:** Identify Nitrogen deficiency by yellowing that begins on older, lower leaves while newer growth remains green.
- **Color Specifics:** 
    - **Potassium:** Look for "burn" patterns (yellowing/browning at tips/margins) or purplish-pink discoloration.
    - **Phosphorus:** Look for deep purple, maroon, or unnaturally dark bluish-green hues.
- **Structural Imbalances:** Use stunted fruit/grain development or cupping of the youngest top leaves to detect Boron and Calcium deficiencies.
- **Pattern Differentiation:** Distinguish broad, diffuse yellowing (deficiency) from sharp, irregular light-and-dark green "islands" (Mosaic virus).

### Healthy
- **Senescence vs. Deficiency:** Recognize that yellowing confined strictly to the oldest, lowest leaves is often natural senescence, not deficiency.
- **Genetic Traits:** Recognize that purple/reddish pigmentation in stems or veins can be a cultivar trait or temperature response rather than Phosphorus deficiency.
- **Abiotic Stress:** Do not diagnose drought based on cracked soil if the foliage remains upright and green.
- **Morphology:** Do not mistake naturally deeply lobed leaf edges for insect chewing holes.
